\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}
Whether Earth-like conditions on other planets in our galaxy are common is one central question in modern astronomy, which observational data itself has not been able to answer yet. The discovery of thousands of exoplanets in the last two decades have shown that small planets are ubiquitous around all types of dwarf stars \parencite{Fressin2013,Bergsten2022}, with an occurrence-rate distribution that is bimodal across the Fulton radius valley near $1.8\,\Rearth$ separating rocky
super-Earths from sub-Neptunes \parencite{Fulton2017}, and that
$\eta_\oplus\sim 0.2$--$0.6$ rocky planets sit in the classically-defined
habitable zone of each solar-like host star \parencite{Bryson2021,Kunimoto2020}.
Yet the observational sample of planets receiving near-Earth irradiation remains sparse, biased toward large radii, and with the exception of a small number of bright nearby M dwarfs dominated by hosts, too faint for the atmospheric follow-up that a habitability claim would require \parencite{Bean2017,LustigYaeger2019}. The habitable zone therefore remains, more than three decades after its modern formulation \parencite{Kasting1988,Kopparapu2013}, a theoretical construct grounded on essentially a single data point.


A clear illusatration of this comes from Venus and Earth. Both accreted from overlapping mass reservoirs
\parencite{Kleine2020,Raymond2020}, exhibit bulk densities within a
few percent of each other \parencite{Smrekar2018}, and are thought to have undergo similar formation stages.
However, their current states differ dramatically. Whether Venus lost its water content in the past still remains open \parencite{Hamano2013,Turbet2021}.

The runaway greenhouse effect is thought to separate them: If a planet's irradiation exceeds the threshold, where it is higher than the outgoing longwave radiation, no stable state of liquid surface water is possible and the entire ocenas evaporate into a steam atmosphere, leading to a net radius inflation. This observation, that the runaway transition is imprinted on the transit radius reframes the question of habitable zone. Instead of attempting to prove per-planet if it is habitable, we aim to aks whether the whole \emph{population} of small planets this step in mean transit radius.
A statistical detection of this discontinuity would provide a first observational test of the habitable zone concept itself, whereas a non-detection would deliver an upper limit on water content in the population of small planets \parencite{Turbet2019,BixelApai2020,Bean2017}.
\textcite{Schlecker2024} formalised this idea into a concrete
predictive framework, which terming the habitable-zone inner edge
discontinuity, or \HZIED.






\section{Aims and contributions of this thesis}
\label{sec:contributions}

This thesis extends the \HZIED framework of \textcite{Schlecker2024}
along with an evaluation on actual \PLATO targets.


\emph{Real target samples.} The planet population generator ingests the PLATO Input Catalogue directly and produces planet catalogues for the P4 (M-dwarf, $V\leq 16$) and P5 (FGK, $V\leq 13$) samples defined
by \textcite{Nascimbeni2022}. Per-sample radius precisions are
adopted from the \PLATO \emph{Definition Study Report}
\parencite{ESA2017PLATO} rather than assumed to be uniform across
the survey.

\emph{Real stellar ages}
Where the original framework drew stellar
ages from a uniform prior on $[0,\,10]\,\mathrm{Gyr}$, this thesis
uses the Gaia DR3 FLAME isochrone catalogue \parencite{GaiaDR3}
directly, or a bootstrap fill from the FLAME distribution when no
FLAME value is available, or a blend of FLAME plus a uniform prior
for the unmeasured tail.

\emph{Physically extended steam prescriptions.}  Beyond the
\textcite{Turbet2020} grey-atmosphere table used in the
original framework, additional prescriptions are compared side
by side: a AGNI radiative-convective grid, an
AGNI+PALEOS-updated version of that grid, the
\textcite{Aguichine2021} irradiated-ocean model, the
\textcite{Pierrehumbert2023} Hycean prescription, and a mixed
rocky/Hycean population motivated by \textcite{Innes2023}.





The overarching question addressed by these extensions is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Can, and if so, when during mission time can \PLATO detect the population-level radius discontinuity predicted by the runaway greenhouse transition, and which combination of stellar age distribution, planetary composition, and observed target sample is required to make the detection decisive?}
\end{quote}
